1. Graphic (Freehand) Method

The simplest method: plot the data on a graph (time on X-axis, values on Y-axis) and draw a smooth freehand line through the points showing the general direction.

Assumption

The trend is assumed to be smooth and gradual (a straight line or gentle curve). Short-term ups and downs (seasonal, cyclical, irregular) are treated as deviations around this line and are balanced out: points above the line roughly equal points below, and the line should follow the general direction, not each point.

Steps

  1. Plot the original time series values on graph paper.
  2. Look at the overall movement, ignoring small ups and downs.
  3. Draw a smooth line (or curve) so that it passes through the middle of the data, with about equal points above and below.
  4. Read estimated trend values from the line for each year.
  5. Estimate or forecast for any year: extend the trend line if needed, go up from that year on the X-axis to the line (dashed guide), then across to the Y-axis and read the value. Forecasts are reliable only for the near future and if the trend continues.

Merits & Demerits

  • Merits: simple, quick, needs no calculation, flexible for linear and non-linear data.
  • Demerits: subjective – different people draw different lines; not suitable for forecasting accurately.
Exam tip: Always label axes, give a title, and use a scale starting from a suitable origin.

2. Semi-Average Method

The data are divided into two equal parts, the average of each part is found, and these averages are plotted at the mid-points of their parts. A straight line through them is the trend.

Steps

  1. Divide the series into two equal halves. If the number of years is odd, leave out the middle year.
  2. Find the average of each half (A₁ and A₂).
  3. Place A₁ and A₂ against the middle year of each half.
  4. Slope b = (A₂ − A₁) ÷ (gap between mid-points in years).
  5. Trend: Y = A₁ + b(t − mid₁); forecast by putting the required year.
b = (A₂ − A₁) / (t₂ − t₁)

Merits: easy, objective. Demerits: assumes linear trend; affected by extreme values.

3. Moving Average Method

Trend values are the averages of successive groups of m years, shifting forward one year at a time. This smooths out short-term fluctuations.

Odd period (3, 5)

3-yr MA = (Y₁+Y₂+Y₃)/3 → placed against middle year
  • Place each average opposite the middle year.
  • Lose (m−1)/2 values at each end.

Even period (4, 6)

  1. Find moving totals of m values, placed between two years.
  2. Find 2-item moving totals of these (centering).
  3. Divide by 2m to get the centred trend value against the actual year.
4-yr centred MA = (2-item total of 4-yr totals) / 8
Period of m years: you lose m−1 trend values in total. Choose m equal to the cycle length for best smoothing.

4. Method of Least Squares

Fits the straight line Y = a + bX such that the sum of squared deviations of actual values from trend values is minimum (ΣY−Yc)² = least.

Normal equations

ΣY = na + bΣX
ΣXY = aΣX + bΣX²

Short-cut: take origin at the middle year

Then ΣX = 0, so the equations reduce to:

a = ΣY / n     b = ΣXY / ΣX²
  • Odd n: X = year − middle year (…−2, −1, 0, 1, 2…).
  • Even n: X = year − mid-point of two middle years, in half-year units (−3, −1, 1, 3…; then b is per half-year, double it for yearly).

Forecasting

Substitute the value of X for the required year in Yc = a + bX.

Merits: mathematical, objective, gives trend for all years, allows forecasting. Demerits: assumes linear trend; adding data needs recalculation.

5. Weighted Price Index Numbers

A price index measures the change in prices of a group of items between a base year (0) and a current year (1). Weighted indices use quantities as weights.

p₀, p₁ = base/current price   q₀, q₁ = base/current quantity

Laspeyres' index (base-year weights)

P₀₁ = (Σp₁q₀ / Σp₀q₀) × 100

Easy, needs quantities of base year only. Tends to overestimate (substitution bias).

Paasche's index (current-year weights)

P₀₁ = (Σp₁q₁ / Σp₀q₁) × 100

Reflects current buying habits but needs new quantities every year. Tends to underestimate.

Fisher's Ideal index

Geometric mean of Laspeyres and Paasche:

P₀₁ = √(L × P) = √[(Σp₁q₀/Σp₀q₀) × (Σp₁q₁/Σp₀q₁)] × 100

Called “ideal” because it satisfies both the time reversal and factor reversal tests.

Kelley's index (fixed weights)

P₀₁ = (Σp₁q / Σp₀q) × 100

q = quantities of any fixed period (given in the question: a chosen year, or an average of several years). Use the separate Kelley calculator below. One way to get q: (q₀+q₁)/2.

Tests of adequacy

  • Time reversal test: P₀₁ × P₁₀ = 1
  • Factor reversal test: P₀₁ × Q₀₁ = Σp₁q₁ / Σp₀q₀ (price index × quantity index = value ratio). Q₀₁ is found by interchanging p and q in the formula.
Fisher's formula passes both tests. Laspeyres and Paasche fail both. Remember: do the calculation as ratios first, multiply by 100 at the end.

Formula Sheet

Quick revision of all methods.

Trend: Graphic

Plot Y against time, draw a smooth line through the middle of the points, extend it to forecast.

Trend: Semi-average

A₁ = ΣY(first half)/k   A₂ = ΣY(second half)/k
b = (A₂ − A₁)/(t₂ − t₁)
Y = A₁ + b(t − t₁)

Odd number of years: leave out the middle year.

Trend: Moving average

Odd m: MA = (m-year total)/m, placed at the middle year
Even m: centred MA = (2-item total of m-year totals)/(2m)

Trend values lost = m − 1 in total.

Trend: Least squares

Y = a + bX
ΣY = na + bΣX
ΣXY = aΣX + bΣX²
If ΣX = 0: a = ΣY/n, b = ΣXY/ΣX²

Odd n: X = year − middle year. Even n: X in half-year units (−3, −1, 1, 3…).

Price index numbers (base 0, current 1)

Laspeyres = Σp₁q₀ / Σp₀q₀ × 100
Paasche = Σp₁q₁ / Σp₀q₁ × 100
Fisher = √(Laspeyres × Paasche)
Kelley = Σp₁q / Σp₀q × 100   (q = fixed quantities)

Tests of adequacy

Time reversal: P₀₁ × P₁₀ = 1
Factor reversal: P₀₁ × Q₀₁ = Σp₁q₁ / Σp₀q₀

Fisher satisfies both. Laspeyres and Paasche satisfy neither. Laspeyres tends to overestimate, Paasche to underestimate.

Q1. Semi-average method

Sales (₹ lakh) for 2015 to 2020: 10, 12, 14, 18, 21, 24. Fit a trend by the semi-average method and forecast 2023.

Show solution

First half (2015–17): A₁ = (10+12+14)/3 = 12 at 2016.
Second half (2018–20): A₂ = (18+21+24)/3 = 21 at 2019.

b = (21 − 12)/(2019 − 2016) = 3 per year.

Y = 12 + 3(t − 2016)

2023: 12 + 3(7) = 33.

Q3. 4-year centred moving average

Values for 2014 to 2019: 30, 34, 38, 36, 44, 48. Find the 4-year centred moving averages.

Show solution

4-year totals: 138 (2015–16), 152 (2016–17), 166 (2017–18).
2-item totals: 138+152 = 290 (at 2016), 152+166 = 318 (at 2017).

Divide by 8: 2016 → 36.25, 2017 → 39.75.

Q4. Least squares

Profit for 2016 to 2020: 8, 10, 13, 15, 19. Fit a straight line Y = a + bX and estimate 2023.

Show solution

Origin 2018, X = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2.
ΣY = 65, ΣXY = −16 − 10 + 0 + 15 + 38 = 27, ΣX² = 10.

a = 65/5 = 13, b = 27/10 = 2.7

Y = 13 + 2.7X

2023: X = 5, so Y = 13 + 13.5 = 26.5.
